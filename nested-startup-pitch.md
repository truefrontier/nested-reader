# Nested

## Finally understand what AI wrote.

**Open your AI-generated plans, research, and Markdown docs. Ask about any passage. Follow the reasoning. Go as deep as you need.**

*01 / The idea — A founding vision*

Your AI has finished the plan. It looks thorough. The terminology sounds right. Now someone needs to decide whether to build it.

That someone is you.

You recognize the destination, but parts of the route are unfamiliar. Why this architecture? What does that term mean? Which assumptions matter? Could you explain the recommendation to a colleague—or recognize where it might fail?

**Nested helps you turn AI-generated work into understanding you can use.**

Open the document you already have. Select the first passage that gives you pause. Ask your question where it arose. A clarification appears beside the text. A deeper question opens another page. The explanations stay connected to the material that prompted them, so you can follow an idea without losing your place.

As you explore, the document grows into a personal book: the original work, the evidence you examined, the alternatives you considered, and the explanations that made it click.

The immediate job is practical. Understand a plan before approving it. Examine research before relying on it. Learn enough about a proposed system to help build and maintain it.

The larger idea is expansive: **what you read should be able to grow in the direction of your questions.**

AI-generated documents give that idea a compelling starting point. They put detailed material directly in front of people who need to evaluate it. Nested will meet readers at that moment and help them develop the understanding the work demands.

Start with the document your AI just wrote. Find the sentence you cannot yet explain. Open the next useful layer.

# Your agent wrote the plan. Follow the reasoning.

*02 / The experience*

Imagine your coding agent has drafted an implementation plan for a new product. It recommends a queue, background workers, and something called *idempotency*.

You open the plan in Nested.

Select the unfamiliar term. Ask, “What does this mean in our product?” A short explanation appears beside the passage, grounded in the plan and the project material you provide. You keep reading.

Now ask, “What happens if the same job runs twice?” A new page walks through the scenario. A diagram makes the sequence visible. You can inspect the assumptions and follow the parts that need more explanation.

The recommendation raises a bigger question: “Do we need this complexity yet?” Nested opens a branch that examines the proposed approach, a simpler alternative, and the conditions that would favor each. Supporting references remain available; unresolved claims stay visible.

You can pause there or go deeper. Ask for a worked example. Explore a failure case. Save a question about operations for tomorrow. Every branch retains its connection to the original plan.

Before returning to your agent, you ask for a review brief: what you understand, what you want changed, and what still needs evidence. The brief links back to the relevant passages and investigations.

When the agent revises the plan, Nested will help you compare versions and identify which explanations or conclusions need another look.

That is the core interaction:

**Open the output → question a passage → follow the explanation → decide what to do.**

The next useful layer might be a definition, a diagram, a counterargument, a small experiment, or a whole chapter. Its form follows the question. The result is a document you can discuss, challenge, and act on with a clearer view of its reasoning.

# Every explanation should help you examine the work.

*03 / The product thesis*

Nested's organizing unit is a question in context: the passage that prompted it, the evidence that helped answer it, and the next question it opened.

Each exchange contributes to a navigable document. Readers can return to the original claim, compare alternative explanations, or pick up an unfinished investigation. The reading experience preserves the relationships between these pieces as the material grows.

Four commitments will shape the product.

### Explain at the depth the reader needs

Move from a plain explanation to the underlying mechanism, a worked example, and the exceptions. Nested will adapt to the reader's questions and stated experience. Visual explanations and interactive examples can make a difficult idea easier to inspect.

### Make the evidence visible

An AI-generated plan is material to examine. Its claims still need support. Nested will distinguish the original output, cited evidence, generated explanations, and the reader's own conclusions. Readers should be able to inspect a supporting passage and see when a claim remains unresolved.

A second fluent explanation cannot establish that the first one was correct. The product must help readers find corroborating evidence, surface conflicting accounts, and recognize missing information.

### Let understanding prove itself

Readers can explain an idea in their own words, predict an outcome, test an assumption, or apply a concept to a new example. These optional exercises can reveal gaps and guide the next explanation. The reader controls how much guidance they want.

### Preserve how the thinking changed

Keep questions attached to their context. Preserve revisions and alternative paths. Make the work editable and exportable, with clear control over private and shared material. When a source document changes, show which parts of the inquiry may need review.

These commitments support a specific promise: help someone understand what an AI produced well enough to explain it, evaluate it, and decide what comes next. Over time, that work becomes a personal library of subjects they have explored in depth.

# Built for the person responsible for the result.

*04 / The first customer and the business*

Our first customer already uses AI to produce substantial work and needs to stand behind the result: a founder directing a coding agent, a developer reviewing a proposed design, or a product lead evaluating generated research.

The strongest early fit combines three conditions: AI is part of their regular workflow, the output reaches beyond their current expertise, and they are responsible for what happens next.

Their job is specific:

> “Help me understand what my AI produced well enough to challenge it, explain it, and use it.”

This gives Nested a natural entry point. The reader arrives with a document and a reason to care. Their first success is understanding a passage that was blocking them. The next is using that understanding to improve a decision, ask their agent a better question, or explain the plan to someone else.

The product should fit into that existing workflow. Opening a Markdown file, pasting a response, or bringing in a generated report should lead directly into reading. A personal book can develop from that first document as the reader's questions accumulate.

Our commercial hypothesis is a paid personal workspace, with a free first document that demonstrates the complete experience. An initial price to test is **$15 per month**, including a clear allowance for AI use. Intensive research can carry transparent limits and optional additional capacity.

The value to validate is better review and continuing understanding across successive documents. Renewal will depend on readers choosing Nested again when the next plan or report arrives.

Team subscriptions could extend the experience to shared review: colleagues examining the same proposal, preserving open questions, and contributing reviewed explanations. Each person can explore at their own depth while keeping the team's reasoning connected.

This is a focused starting market with a broad underlying need. Whenever people delegate the production of information, they still need a way to understand and take responsibility for its use.

# Meet people where the AI hands off the work.

*05 / Distribution, advantage, and ambition*

Nested's first distribution opportunity is the moment a generated document is ready to read.

The proposed integrations are simple invitations: **Open in Nested** beside a coding agent's plan, a research tool's report, or a Markdown file in an editor. The document becomes the starting point. One useful question demonstrates the experience.

We would launch with short, concrete demonstrations built around familiar artifacts: an implementation plan, an architecture proposal, a research brief. Show a reader examining a confusing recommendation, uncovering an assumption, and returning to the work with a better question or a clearer decision.

The first validation would follow 30 people using AI on active projects over four weeks. Can they explain the rationale behind a recommendation? Identify an unsupported assumption? Recover the evidence behind a conclusion? Do they bring their next document to Nested without a reminder, and will they pay to continue?

These are proposed experiments. Their results should guide the business.

Sharing could create a second path to discovery. Someone sends a useful explanation with its source context. The recipient opens it and follows a different question. One person's investigation becomes another person's starting point, with deliberate controls over what is shared.

The long-term advantage would come from the quality of the whole experience: connecting documents to questions, keeping evidence inspectable, helping readers test their understanding, and preserving continuity as the work changes. A library of prior inquiry gives Nested context to serve each reader better; portable files keep that relationship voluntary.

The ambition reaches beyond AI-generated work. Any article, book, course, or organizational document could become a starting point for responsive exploration. Authors could publish material readers develop in their own directions. Teams could maintain living explanations of complex systems.

**A personal book that grows with your curiosity begins with one document you want to understand.**
