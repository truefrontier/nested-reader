# Slow oscillations

Slow oscillations are large, slow waves of cortical activity, below one cycle per second, that dominate deep sleep. Each wave has an up state, when neurons are active, and a down state, when the cortex goes quiet.

They act as the pacemaker for consolidation. Thalamic spindles ride on the rising phase of the up state, and hippocampal ripples ride inside the spindle troughs. This nesting is what times the arrival of replayed sequences to windows when cortical synapses can change.

Strengthening slow oscillations improves memory. In one study, applying a weak oscillating current at the slow-wave frequency during early sleep improved recall of word pairs learned the evening before, while the same current at a faster frequency did nothing.
