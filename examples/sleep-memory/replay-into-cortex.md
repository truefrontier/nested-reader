# Replay into cortex

The cortex learns slowly and the hippocampus learns fast. The standard account says the hippocampus holds a quick index of each episode, then teaches the cortex by replaying it many times while the cortex is offline and not busy with new input.

The teaching happens through timing. Cortical slow oscillations, sleep spindles and hippocampal ripples nest inside one another: a slow wave brings a spindle, and the spindle brings a ripple. When the three line up, cortical synapses are in a receptive state exactly as the hippocampal transcript arrives.

Experiments that strengthen this coupling improve next-day memory, and experiments that break it impair it. Boosting slow oscillations with weak electrical stimulation during sleep improves word-pair recall in people. Disrupting ripples in rats impairs memory for a route learned that day.

What the cortex stores is not the episode itself but a more general version of it. After many nights of replay, the memory becomes less about that particular run through the maze and more about how mazes of that kind tend to go.
