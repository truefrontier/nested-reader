# Targeted memory reactivation

Targeted memory reactivation pairs a memory with a cue while it is learned, then plays the cue again quietly during sleep. A sound heard while learning where an object sat on a screen, played again during slow-wave sleep, improves recall of that object's place the next morning.

The cue seems to bias which memories the sleeping brain replays. Recordings show that the cued memory's activity pattern reappears more often after the cue than before it, and the size of that reappearance predicts how much recall improves.

The effect depends on the sleep stage. Cues during slow-wave sleep help; the same cues during rapid eye movement sleep or wake do little, and cues played too loudly wake the sleeper and undo the benefit.
